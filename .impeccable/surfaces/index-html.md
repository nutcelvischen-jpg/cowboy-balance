# Mobile rodeo surface
Mode: Experience. User explicitly chose a playable version before art iteration; build directly in code. No additional art-selection gate. The Cowboy Jumper picture is thematic evidence, not an approved comp.

## Direction contract
THESIS: The playable arena is the entire screen; the player's own tilted phone is the balance controller. No marketing page or dashboard.
OWN-WORLD: Desert rodeo at late afternoon: teal sky, ochre arena, walnut bull, vermilion bandana, butter-yellow action. A local western display face and clear Chinese control copy. Only one primary action per state.
STORY: See the rider, enable tilt or choose touch, hold naturally to calibrate, survive stronger bucking, recover from a lean, fall and retry.
FIRST VIEWPORT: A small title and sound/pause controls sit at the top. The dimensional bull and cowboy occupy the middle two-thirds, survival time sits at the upper left, and a broad balance bar plus one action sit at the bottom. Desktop expands the same arena, not a separate landing page.
FORM: Candidate 7, the live county-fair rodeo ring; seed c327cdec. Grounded alternatives considered: western game cover, ranch sign, event handbill, leather tack, canyon trail, saloon stage, live rodeo arena. Assigned seventh directly serves code-first play. Challengers (brine calendar, crane, algorave, segment clock, street poster, dance handbill) declined for recognition and gameplay clarity. Keep clear states from brine, continuous movement from crane, pre-cued beats from algorave, legible timing from clock, committed scale from poster, a readable silhouette from handbill. These raise discipline, not borrowed decoration.
FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

The code-first request overrides a separate concept-board approval workflow. Simulation and sensor handling are primary; artistic detail can be tuned after testing on a real phone. No shipping raster is planned: the arena is a real-time 3D scene and all shapes are geometry.
